What I spend my time on

Some of this becomes products. Some of it helps people directly. All of it improves quality of life — mine first, then others.

  • Accessibility & input solutions Controller mapping, voice-driven workflows, alternative input methods — tools for people who interact with computers differently.
  • AI-augmented development A proprietary framework and AI workflow refined over 4 years. The constraint of not being able to type freely forced me to build something genuinely useful.
  • Custom software & automation When someone needs something built, I build it. Dashboards, portals, integrations, workflow tools — whatever solves the actual problem.
  • Sharing what works When something I’ve built for myself could help others, I make it available. Useful software, built with care.
  • Research Embeddings, semantic search, cognitive architecture, pattern recognition — deep work on ideas that might matter tomorrow.

I used to be in love with the world — video games, technology, building things with my hands. Then my hands were taken from me. Bilateral carpal tunnel, bilateral cubital tunnel. The kind of diagnosis that ends careers. But it changed what I see as valuable. The world doesn’t offer me as much as it once did — but I love the people of the world more than I ever did before.
