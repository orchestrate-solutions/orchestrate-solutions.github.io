Why we build

Conducted, not driving.

Every product here started with a problem I lived.
Every service is shaped by what God gave me to share.

I chose the name Orchestrate deliberately — not because I arrange things, but because I’m the instrument. God is my Orchestrator. He leads me, He conducts me, He makes me lie down in green pastures beside quiet waters. He gives me everything I need. This company exists because He put the knowledge in my hands and a need in front of me.

The story

I spent years in love with the world — video games, technology, the thrill of building things. The United States Air Force gave me discipline and scope. eBay gave me scale. Years of self-directed R&D gave me a framework and a way of thinking about software that I couldn’t have bought anywhere. I was doing what I loved, with tools I’d built with my own hands.

Then my hands were taken from me.

Bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome. Bilateral cubital tunnel syndrome. Both arms, both conditions. The kind of diagnosis that ends software careers. The keyboard — the thing I’d used to build everything — became painful to use for any significant stretch of time. Gaming, which I’d loved for years, changed too. The easy, unconscious interaction I’d taken for granted was gone.

When you lose the ability to interact freely, you learn to see the value in every single interaction you do have. You stop taking for granted what it means to touch something — to play a game, to type a sentence, to reach out and help someone. My perspective on what is valuable changed completely. The world doesn’t offer me as much as it once did. But I find that I love the people of the world more than I ever did before.

I still develop software. The controller mapper you’ll find in our Products section was built because I needed it — voice control, AI augmentation, and the right input device configuration are now how I write code. That tool exists because I could not work without it. And because I built it for myself, I know exactly what it needs to be for someone else.

First for me, then for you. Not because I’m selfish, but because the things that actually get polished are the things someone actually uses. Every tool here has been through my hands — or rather, through the accommodations I’ve made because my hands don’t cooperate the way they used to.

A lot of people have knowledge that could change someone’s life — and they keep it because they don’t realize how rare it is. Most people don’t know how to build the tools that would make their own lives easier. They just live with the friction. I want to close that gap. Not because it’s good business — though it is — but because me helping you helps me. Sharing compounds. When I give from what God has put in my hands, something always comes back.

Joshua Wink — Founder, Orchestrate Solutions

What we’re actually trying to do

Not productivity. Not growth hacking. This.

Free you for what matters

Software should reduce friction so you can be more present — with God, your family, your neighbors, the stranger. Those are the valuable parts. Not your output metrics.

Necessity first, always

Every product we ship was built because someone needed it. That person is usually me. Needs-first software gets used. Used software gets polished. Polished software helps people.

Sharing from abundance

God has given me knowledge, tools, time, and perspective. Paul made tents so no one could accuse him of taking advantage — I build software for the same reason. You deserve help from someone with nothing to hide.
