Why Small Businesses Can’t Afford to Skip a Website in 2026

The businesses that are getting your customers aren’t better than you. They’re just easier to find.

The numbers are brutal

About 68% of small businesses still don’t have a website. At the same time, 97% of people search online for local businesses. If you don’t have a website, you are invisible to the majority of potential customers in your area.

Think about how you find a plumber, a dentist, or a restaurant. You Google it. You look at the first few results. You pick one. If a business doesn’t show up, it doesn’t exist in your mind. Your customers do the exact same thing.

Why most small businesses don’t have one

It’s not laziness. It’s sticker shock. The web development industry has priced out its biggest market. Here’s what most businesses encounter:

Provider Typical Cost What You Get
Web agency $5,000 – $15,000+ Custom design, good quality, but expensive and slow
Freelancer (experienced) $3,000 – $8,000 Varies wildly in quality; hard to vet
DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace) $15 – $50/month Looks like a template because it is one; weak SEO
Your nephew who “knows computers” Free – $500 A WordPress site from 2019 with no updates

Small business owners see $5,000+ quotes and walk away. They’ll try the DIY route, get frustrated with a page builder, and settle for a Facebook page as their “website.”

That’s leaving money on the table.

What a website actually does for your business

A professional website isn’t a brochure. It’s a machine that works 24/7:

  • It gets found. Google sends customers to websites, not Facebook pages. A properly built site with SEO shows up when someone searches for what you do.
  • It builds trust. Before they call you, customers look you up. A professional site says “this is a real business that’s been around.”
  • It answers questions. An FAQ page, a services breakdown, hours, pricing — customers get the info they need without calling. The ones who do call are ready to buy.
  • It works while you sleep. Your website takes inquiries at 2 AM, on holidays, when you’re on vacation. A contact form costs $0/hour.

The ROI math

Let’s say you’re a plumber charging $150 per service call. A website that brings in just 2 extra calls per month pays for itself:

  • 2 calls × $150 = $300/month in new revenue
  • $300 × 12 = $3,600/year from a $2,000 investment
  • That’s a 180% return in year one alone

And unlike a newspaper ad or a door flyer, a website keeps working month after month. The SEO compounds — the longer your site is live, the more Google trusts it, the more customers find you.

What to look for when you’re ready

If you decide to get a website (and you should), here’s what matters:

  1. Custom-built, not a template. Templates look like templates. Customers notice.
  2. SEO from day one. A beautiful site nobody finds is a beautiful waste of money.
  3. Mobile-first. Over 60% of searches happen on phones. If your site doesn’t work on mobile, you lost them.
  4. Fast. If it takes more than 3 seconds to load, 53% of visitors leave.
  5. Ongoing support. A website isn’t a one-and-done thing. Content needs updating, SEO needs attention.

The bottom line
